  1. The time in minutes between the last boarding call and take-off for flights offered by a particular airline flying from Lincoln to Denver is a random variable \(X\) with pdf \(f(x;\theta) = e^{-x+\theta}\) for \(x > \theta\) and with CDF \(F(x;\theta) = 1-e^{-x+\theta}\) for \(x > \theta\). For this distribution, \(\theta\) represents the amount of time that must be allocated for the last passengers to board, be seated, and safety announcements made. Assume you have a random sample, \(X_1,\dots, X_n\), from this distribution.

    1. Prove that the pdf of the smallest order statistic, \(X_{(1)}\) is \(f_{X_(1)}(x)=n e^{-xn+\theta n}\) for \(x > \theta\) (and 0 otherwise).

    2. Currently, 15 minutes are allocated for the last passengers to board, be seated, and safety announcements made. The flight attendant union believes that not enough time is allocated, and that an unrealistic value of \(\theta\) is currently being used to determine take-off times. They plan to collect data on 10 randomly selected Lincoln to Denver flights in support of their claim. What hypotheses (in symbols and numbers) is the union interested in testing?

    3. In the context of this scenario, explain what a Type I error would mean to the flight attendant union and to the owners of the airline.

    4. The union proposes using the following test function: \[\begin{eqnarray*} \phi(X_1,\dots, X_n) = \left \{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & X_{(1)} > 15.121 \\ 0 & \hbox{elsewhere} \end{array} \right . \end{eqnarray*}\] Find the probability of a Type I error, assuming the null hypothesis is true.

    5. Based on your responses in (c) and (d), do you think the owners and the union would agree on this test function? Explain your answer.

  2. The Pew Research Center (2013) estimates that 36% of all US adults aged 18-25 have at least 1 tattoo. We suspect that the proportion of UNL students that have at least one tattoo is less than the national value. We decide to test the hypotheses H\(_0: p = 0.36\) versus H\(_a: p < 0.36\). Let the random variable \(\sum_{i=1}^{76} Y_i\) denote the total number (out of the 76 randomly selected UNL students) that have at least 1 tattoo.

    1. Suppose the following two hypothesis test functions \(\phi_1(Y_1,\dots,Y_{76})\) and \(\phi_2(Y_1,\dots, Y_{76})\) are considered: \[ \phi_1(Y_1,\dots, Y_{76}) = \left \{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & \sum_{i=1}^{76} Y_i > c \\ 0 & \hbox{elsewhere} \end{array} \right . \hbox{\hspace{10mm}} \phi_2(Y_1,\dots, Y_{76}) = \left \{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & \sum_{i=1}^{76} Y_i < k \\ 0 & \hbox{elsewhere} \end{array} \right . \] where \(c\) and \(k\) are critical values chosen for a given significance level, \(\alpha\). Explain why, intuitively, we should use the second test function, \(\phi_2(Y_1,\dots,Y_{76}),\) to test the claim, rather than \(\phi_1(Y_1,\dots, Y_{76})\).

    2. Three critical values (20, 25, 30) are proposed for the test function given in (a): \[ \phi_A(\mathbf{Y}) = \left \{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & \sum_{i=1}^{76} Y_i < 20 \\ 0 & \hbox{elsewhere} \end{array} \right . \hbox{\hspace{5mm}} \phi_B(\mathbf{Y}) = \left \{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & \sum_{i=1}^{76} Y_i < 25 \\ 0 & \hbox{elsewhere} \end{array} \right . \hbox{\hspace{5mm}} \phi_C(\mathbf{Y}) = \left \{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & \sum_{i=1}^{76} Y_i < 30 \\ 0 & \hbox{elsewhere} \end{array} \right . \]

      1. Which of these hypothesis test functions always has the smallest probability of making a Type I error for testing the hypotheses given above? Thoroughly explain your rationale in words (Note: Calculated probabilities are not considered valid rationale.)
      2. Which of these hypothesis test functions always has the largest power for testing the hypotheses given above? Thoroughly explain your rationale in words (Note: Calculated probabilities are not considered valid rationale.)
      3. Of the three critical values proposed (20, 25 and 30), which value would you choose for this test? Explain your reasoning.

  3.     3. Derive the likelihood ratio test (LRT) for testing H\(_0: \beta=\beta_0\) versus H\(_a: \beta > \beta_0\). Be sure to show all work and determine the exact critical value required for \(\alpha=0.05\).

What can you conclude?

  1. Let \(X_1, \ldots, X_n\) be a random sample of the total time (from start to finish, in minutes, including non-playing time) of \(n\) basketball games in the men’s NCAA tournament.     2. Find the MLE of \(\theta\).

You decide to test the claims of these articles. What hypotheses (in symbols and numbers) would test of the claims of the articles?
